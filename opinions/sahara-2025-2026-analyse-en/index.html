ANALYSIS • 27 OCTOBER 2026

Western Sahara: a diplomatic shift in Rabat’s favor and a strategic dilemma for Algiers and the Polisario Front

The Western Sahara dispute is entering a major diplomatic turning point. Following Security Council Resolution 2797 (2025), the 2026 US draft places Morocco’s autonomy proposal under Moroccan sovereignty at the center of negotiations. What was previously a basis for talks is now proposed as their only basis, confronting Algeria and the Polisario Front with a strategic dilemma.

Political and legal analysis • Bawaba editorial team

Document status: Resolution 2797 (2025) was formally adopted by the Security Council. The 2026 US text examined here is an unadopted draft circulated in unofficial copies; its wording may change.

2025: the turning point of Resolution 2797

Adopted on 31 October 2025, Resolution 2797 extended MINURSO’s mandate until 31 October 2026. It called for negotiations taking Morocco’s autonomy proposal as their basis and described genuine autonomy as potentially one of the most feasible outcomes, within a mutually acceptable political solution providing for the self-determination of the people of Western Sahara.

2026: from a basis to the only basis for negotiations

The US-backed 2026 draft, discussed in consultations involving the Group of Friends of Western Sahara — the United States, Russia, the United Kingdom, France and Spain — goes further. Its operative paragraphs 5 and 7 present Morocco’s expanded and updated autonomy plan as the only basis for continuing negotiations. Yabiladi has reported on consultations and amendments before the draft’s consideration by the fifteen Council members. This analysis compares only the adopted 2025 resolution with the 2026 draft under examination.

Most feasible solution and only basis: two distinct ideas

Resolution 2797 described genuine autonomy as potentially one of the most feasible outcomes. The 2026 draft describes genuine autonomy under Moroccan sovereignty as the most feasible solution, while treating Morocco’s updated plan as the only basis for talks. The former concerns the envisaged outcome; the latter defines the proposed negotiating framework, alongside agreement between the parties and the principles of the UN Charter, including self-determination.

Rabat’s diplomatic gains

The comparison reveals three developments: growing specificity and prominence for Morocco’s autonomy plan; explicit reference to Moroccan sovereignty in the draft’s description of the most feasible solution; and a proposal to rethink and rename the UN mission originally established in connection with a referendum. These changes come amid growing support among states for autonomy under Moroccan sovereignty.

MINURSO: a proposed institutional transformation

Where Resolution 2797 extended MINURSO and requested a strategic review, the 2026 draft proposes extending its mandate to 31 October 2027 and renaming it the United Nations Mission in Western Sahara (MINUSO) from 1 November 2026. It requests proposals from the Secretary-General by 30 January 2027 concerning personnel, locations, resources and functions, including ceasefire monitoring, mine clearance and support for the political process.

Tindouf: refugee registration and durable solutions

The draft calls on UNHCR to complete individual registration of refugees in the Tindouf region within six months, in line with international law and UNHCR standards, with unhindered access and freedom of movement. Registration could improve knowledge of population numbers and needs. It could also help assess three voluntary durable solutions: repatriation to a country of origin, including Morocco for those who choose and qualify; local integration in Algeria with the consent of the people and authorities concerned; or resettlement in a willing third country. None may be imposed.

Broader Sahrawi consultations

The draft asks the UN Personal Envoy to consult a broad range of Sahrawi voices, including people outside the negotiating parties, in the territory, camps and diaspora, and to report back to the Council. Such consultations could diversify representation and, over time, reduce the Polisario Front’s relative influence as a political interlocutor. They would not in themselves remove its role as a party to negotiations.

First difficult choice: negotiate on the Moroccan plan

If adopted without fundamental changes, the draft would leave Algeria and the Polisario with the option of engaging in negotiations based exclusively on Morocco’s plan. Participation would allow discussion of regional powers, institutions, rights and oversight, but could be interpreted politically as acceptance of a framework the Polisario contests. Agreeing to negotiate does not necessarily mean endorsing the outcome in advance.

Second difficult choice: reject the framework

Alternatively, the parties could reject the proposed basis, suspend talks or demand substantial changes. That approach would preserve the Polisario’s independence claim, but could diminish its ability to shape negotiations backed by Washington and potentially by the Council. Any diplomatic consequences would depend on the final text and the positions taken by Council members.

Conclusion: a new diplomatic equation for Algeria and the Polisario

If the 2026 US draft were adopted as currently worded, Algeria and the Polisario would face a negotiating framework centered more firmly on autonomy under Moroccan sovereignty. Algeria would confront both a diplomatic challenge and new expectations regarding refugee registration in Tindouf. Broader consultations could diversify Sahrawi representation, while the proposed transformation of MINURSO into MINUSO would signal an institutional shift. The central choice would be whether to engage within the proposed Moroccan-plan framework or reject it and risk reduced influence over the process. For Rabat, adoption would strengthen the place of its autonomy initiative in UN-led diplomacy.
