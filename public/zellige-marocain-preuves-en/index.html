Moroccan Zellij: historical, textual and material evidence

From early ceramic paving in Fez to Almohad Marrakech, fourteenth-century texts and modern archives: a documentary dossier on Moroccan zellij.

The strongest case is the convergence of dated texts, monuments, craft vocabulary, production centres and living transmission.

This page distinguishes glazed ceramic, tile, mosaic and cut-and-assembled zellij. Ancient glazed ware is not automatically zellij.

UNESCO conformity test: criteria R.1–R.5

Procedural rule: for the Representative List, the submitting State must demonstrate that the element satisfies all criteria R.1 to R.5. The mere presence of a nomination form, photographs, consent documents or an inventory extract does not by itself establish substantive conformity. Official UNESCO criteria.

R.1 — A living, identifiable and precisely defined element

This is the principal vulnerability. File 02280 groups under “zellidje, glazed ceramics” Hammadid tesserae from Béjaïa, medieval decoration in Tlemcen and contemporary practice in Tipasa. Ancient architectural ceramics do not by themselves prove that these materials belong to the same living element or to one transmitted technical chain.

Key test: comparable operational sequence, cutting and assembly principles, tools, terminology, modes of apprenticeship and documented continuity. Without that demonstration, R.1 remains insufficiently established.

R.2 — Visibility, dialogue and cultural diversity

R.2 requires inscription to encourage dialogue and reflect cultural diversity. Where zellij is documented across several parts of the Maghreb and al-Andalus, the nomination should distinguish local forms and acknowledge the shared regional context rather than imply exclusivity over a broad regional concept.

R.3 — Safeguarding a living practice

Safeguarding measures should protect transmission through masters, apprentices, workshops, gestures, geometric knowledge and the production chain. Monument restoration or commercial promotion alone is not equivalent to safeguarding living intangible heritage.

R.4 — Community participation and consent

UNESCO's portal indicates that community consents were submitted, so R.4 should not be described as absent. The relevant question is qualitative: do the signatories represent the diversity of practitioners, and did communities participate in defining the element itself?

R.5 — Consistency with the national inventory

An inventory extract is present, but the name, technical definition, bearer communities and geographical scope in the inventory should correspond to the element nominated as “zellidje”. If the inventory covers a broader glazed-ceramics category, that correspondence requires scrutiny.

Procedural conclusion: if the file does not coherently demonstrate that Hammadid tesserae, medieval Tlemcen ensembles and contemporary practice constitute one precisely defined living element called zellidje, non-inscription in its current form would follow the logic of the criteria. If the gaps are considered remediable, referral for clarification would allow a more precise definition, proof of technical continuity and fuller recognition of the shared regional context.
Additional request to the Committee — defer a final decision: given the remaining ambiguities concerning the technical definition of the element, the continuity between Hammadid evidence, medieval Tlemcen ensembles and contemporary practice, and the consistency between the nominated scope and the national inventory, the Committee is requested not to take a final inscription decision at this stage. The most appropriate procedural course would be to refer the nomination to the submitting State for additional information concerning the operational chain, intergenerational transmission, bearer communities, technical terminology and the precise scope of the element. A final decision should be taken only after those clarifications have been examined.

Modern protection

The “Zellige de Fès” certification mark protects provenance and manufacturing standards; it is not an international ruling of exclusive ownership over all zellij.
